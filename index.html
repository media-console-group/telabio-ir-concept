TELA Bio · Nasdaq: TELA · Malvern, Pennsylvania

Reinforced tissue matrices for soft-tissue reconstruction

TELA Bio sells OviTex, layers of ovine rumen tissue with polymer thread embroidered through them, for hernia repair and abdominal wall reconstruction, and OviTex PRS for plastic and reconstructive surgery. The company says its aim is the “preservation and restoration of the patient’s own anatomy.” 10-K, Item 1

Product

A layered tissue matrix with polymer thread stitched through it

The company’s technology platform, in its own description, is “multiple layers of minimally-processed, decellularized extracellular matrix derived from ovine rumen with interwoven polymer fibers in a unique embroidered pattern.” 10-K, Item 1

The tissue is the collagen component of the rumen, the forestomach of a sheep, kept after the lining, muscle and cells are removed. The thread is either permanent (polypropylene) or resorbable (PGA or PLGA). It is sewn in a lockstitch, which the company says lets the device be trimmed with less unraveling, and the embroidery creates “hundreds of micro-channels” for the patient’s own cells and fluids to pass through. 10-K, Item 1

OviTex is sold for hernia repair and abdominal wall reconstruction. It was first sold in the U.S. in July 2016 and in Europe in February 2019. OviTex PRS, for plastic and reconstructive surgery, was first sold in the U.S. in May 2019. The OviTex products are made for the company by Aroa Biosurgery in Auckland, New Zealand, under an exclusive license. 10-K, Item 1

The company reports “over sixty-five” published or presented works on its clinical findings. Its single-arm post-market study, BRAVO, enrolled 92 patients with primary or recurrent ventral hernias. The recurrence rate at the 24-month time point was 2.6%, and surgical site occurrences were seen in 38% of the study population; 78% of the enrolled patients were characterized as high risk for them. 10-K, Item 1

OviTex PRS

OviTex PRS comes in three options of two or three layers. The short-term resorbable and permanent options are joined by an embroidered diamond-pattern polymer (PGA or polypropylene) that lets the product stretch in one direction; the long-term resorbable option stretches in two directions and resorbs more slowly. 10-K, Item 1

It is cleared by the FDA to reinforce soft tissue in plastic and reconstructive surgery. The company received approval of an investigational device exemption in October 2024 for a study in implant-based breast reconstruction, and says it is finalizing the study protocol and expects further FDA interactions to support a pre-market application for a breast reconstruction indication. The 10-K’s risk factors say the FDA will require a PMA, rather than a 510(k) clearance, for that use. 10-K, Item 1 10-K, Item 1A

Four things the company points to

  1. 01

    Less permanent polymer

    Synthetic material is about 5% of the OviTex Core, 1S and 2S products and about 13% of LPR and IHR. Depending on the configuration, the company puts that at about 75% less polymer than the most widely implanted permanent synthetic mesh. 10-K, Item 1

  2. 02

    Published data

    BRAVO’s 24-month results were published in October 2022 in the Annals of Medicine and Surgery. Both rates are given above: 2.6% recurrence and 38% surgical site occurrences. 10-K, Item 1

  3. 03

    Price

    The company says OviTex is sold at prices about 20% to 40% lower than other biologic matrices and resorbable synthetic mesh, and notes an established fixed-payment (MS-DRG) reimbursement pathway for hernia repair. 10-K, Item 1

  4. 04

    Patents and supply

    Its patents claim the ability to tailor stretch resistance by interweaving polymer thread through a biologic matrix. Aroa supplies the ovine rumen and makes the products; the company pays Aroa a transfer cost equal to 27% of net sales of OviTex and OviTex PRS (for OviTex IHR, the greater of a fixed transfer cost or 27%). The company lists its dependence on Aroa as a risk factor. 10-K, Item 1 10-K, Item 1A

New clearance

Liora Monofilament Scaffold. On Oct 5, 2026 the company announced FDA 510(k) clearance for Liora, which it describes as “a fully resorbable surgical mesh device for soft tissue reinforcement” and the first implant fully developed in its ISO 8 certified cleanroom in Malvern, Pennsylvania. The company said it would give more detail on its third quarter earnings call, expected in early November. No sales of Liora are reported. 8-K, Oct 5, 2026

Market

About 1.2 million U.S. hernia repairs a year, by the company’s count

The company calls hernia repair “one of the most common surgeries performed in the U.S.” and says, without naming a source, that it is estimated that about 90% of repairs use some form of reconstruction material, either synthetic mesh or a biologic matrix made from tissue. 10-K, Item 1

What the filings say about the company’s position

Set out in the filed words where the words matter. Nothing on this page replaces the filings, and the filings are linked beside each item.

01

Going concern

In the 10-Q for the quarter ended Jun 30, 2026, filed Aug 11, 2026, management concluded:

These conditions and events raise substantial doubt about the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern within one year after the date these consolidated financial statements are issued. 10-Q, Note 3

The note cites recurring losses and negative cash flows from operations since inception, with an accumulated deficit of $421.1M, and says “management’s current forecasts indicate it is probable that the Company will not achieve the minimum revenue threshold required under the Credit Agreement during the twelve-month period following the issuance of these consolidated financial statements.” It adds that “projected compliance with the liquidity covenant is sensitive to changes in the assumptions of our operating forecast.” The 10-Q says the company was in compliance with its covenants at Jun 30, 2026, and that if it failed to meet them without a waiver or amendment, the lender “could declare an event of default and accelerate repayment,” which the company “does not expect to have sufficient liquidity” to repay. 10-Q, Note 3

Management’s stated plans are to seek a waiver or amendment, to pursue initiatives to increase revenue and to reduce operating expenses. The note says such actions “would not eliminate the risk of noncompliance with the revenue covenant,” and Item 1A adds that “there can be no assurances” a waiver or amendment will be obtained on acceptable terms, or at all. 10-Q, Note 3 10-Q, Item 1A

The 10-K for 2025 contained no going-concern conclusion. No filing since the 10-Q has updated this one; the Sep 14 proxy statement says only that if the company cannot raise sufficient additional capital it “may not be able to continue as a going concern.” DEF 14A, Sep 14, 2026 On Sep 28 the lenders agreed the minimum revenue covenant would not be tested for the quarters ending on or about Sep 30 and Dec 31, 2026, two of the quarters in the twelve-month period the 10-Q assessed; the consent’s full text is to be filed with the 10-Q for the quarter ended Sep 30, 2026. 8-K, Sep 29, 2026

The same 10-Q’s liquidity overview states that, on the current business plan, the company believes its existing cash resources “will be sufficient to meet our capital requirements and fund our operations for at least the next 12 months.” Both statements are in the report. 10-Q, Liquidity

  1. 02

    The Perceptive term loan

    $60.0M of a senior secured term loan facility of up to $70.0M with Perceptive Credit Holdings V, LP was funded on Nov 14, 2025 and matures on Nov 14, 2030. There are no scheduled principal payments before maturity. Interest is 7.85% plus the greater of a reference rate and 4.25%, paid monthly, and prepayment carries a premium of 2% to 10% of the principal prepaid, depending on the date. The loan is secured by a first lien on substantially all of the company’s assets. 10-Q, Note 6

    The company must keep liquidity of at least $5.0M at all times and, as of each quarterly calculation date, revenue not below amounts set in the credit agreement; the amounts are not stated in the 10-Q. Events of default include a covenant violation; after one the applicable margin rises by a further 3.00% a year, and the lender could accelerate the loan (the 10-Q’s risk factor adds that it could foreclose on the collateral). The lender also holds warrants for 2,000,000 shares at $1.11 that expire on Nov 14, 2035. 10-Q, Note 6 10-Q, Item 1A

  5. 06

    Leadership and workforce

    On Aug 3 the board determined that Antony Koblish, the chief executive and a co-founder, would no longer serve as chief executive; he also resigned from the board. The chief financial and chief operating officer, Roberto Cuca, stepped down on Aug 31. Both are treated as terminations without cause. Heather Getz is chief executive and principal financial officer. The chairman, Doug Evans, and three other directors said on Apr 29 that they would leave the board after the Jun 9 annual meeting; Joseph Capper, elected at that meeting, was to become chairman, and three directors were appointed to take seats after it. 8-K, Aug 4, 2026 8-K, Mar 24, 2026 8-K, Aug 31, 2026 8-K, Apr 30, 2026 8-K, Jun 11, 2026

    The board approved on Aug 28 a reduction of about 20% of the workforce, from 201 to 160 full-time employees, with aggregate charges of about $1.5M expected in the third quarter, primarily severance and other employee-related costs that it expects to be paid in cash. In its Aug 31 release the company said it had taken actions to reduce annual operating expenses by about $17.0M, through the headcount reduction and the streamlining of external resources, and expected “to extend our cash runway into 2028.” That is a forward-looking statement in a press release; the going-concern statement in item 01 is the one made in the financial statements. 8-K, Aug 31, 2026

  6. 07

    One manufacturer

    Aroa Biosurgery is the exclusive contract manufacturer of OviTex and OviTex PRS, in New Zealand, and the 10-K lists the company’s dependence on it as a risk factor. The FDA clearances for the OviTex hernia products were obtained and are held by Aroa, not by TELA Bio; the OviTex PRS clearances are held by TELA Bio. The Aroa license runs to the expiry of the last covered patent, currently Mar 9, 2031, with an option to extend for ten years. Liora, cleared Oct 5, is the first implant developed in the company’s own cleanroom. 10-K, Item 1A 10-K, Item 1 8-K, Oct 5, 2026
